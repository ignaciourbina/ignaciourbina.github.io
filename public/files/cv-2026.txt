Three Essays on the Political Implications of AI. Committee: Oleg Smirnov (Chair), Reuben Kline, Andrew Engelhardt.

1. Ignacio Urbina. The Conditional Activation Model: Uncovering Policy Demand Responses to AI-Driven
   Automation (Job Market Paper).
   • Introduces a two-gate framework explaining when citizens update policy preferences in response to AI-driven
      job automation risk. Pre-registered experiment with 901 U.S. workers; trust-stratum effects recovered by entropy
      balancing on pre-treatment covariates.
2. Ignacio Urbina. Simulating Open Democracy: An Agent-Based Framework for Testing Deliberative Institu-
   tional Designs.
   • Open-source simulation using LLM agents as synthetic citizens to test how different institutional rules impact
      democratic consensus. Presented at the 2026 MPSA Meeting.
3. Ignacio Urbina & Oleg Smirnov. One Dimension or Many? The Structure of Artificial Intelligence Attitudes
   in Mass Publics.
   • Maps the structure of U.S. public attitudes toward AI with exploratory factor analysis on a nationally representative
      sample (N = 5,153): fifty-two items resolve into ten factors organized by the social context of deployment, a belief-
      system network with no general-attitude hub, and demographic cleavages that reverse direction across dimensions.

Working Papers

1. Ignacio Urbina & Vitória Sgorlon. Fractured Trust: The Spillover Effects of Police Violence on Satisfaction
   with Democracy and Political Trust.
   • Three studies: two-wave survey experiment in Brazil (N = 1,004), observational analysis of Chile’s 2019 protest
     repression, and cross-national LAPOP analysis of sixteen Latin American countries.
   • Presented at the 2024 Society of Political Methodology Conference, the 2024 Human Rights Measurement Work-
     shop at the University of Rhode Island, the 2024 MPSA Meeting, and the 2024 APSA Meeting.
   • Winner of the Best Poster Award at the 2023 Latin American Political Methodology Meeting.
   • Awarded with the 2022 Stony Brook University Fine Arts, Humanities, and Social Sciences Research Grant
     ($4,000).
2. Ignacio Urbina. Automation Risk, Redistribution, and Economic Vulnerability: Evidence from the European
   Social Survey, 2002–2022
   • Rebuilds and extends the Thewissen and Rueda (2019) design across all eleven European Social Survey waves
     (more than 270,000 respondents in 35 countries). The automation risk association with redistribution preferences
     reappears in 2002–2012 but is smaller in the following decade, with wide country-year variation.
   • Welfare and labor market institutions condition the exposure–insecurity link: union density weakens it, while net
     income inequality and strict employment protection strengthen it.
3. Ignacio Urbina. Repressive Democracies: The Relationship Between State’s Repression Severity, Protests, and
   Policy Change.
   • A global game of policy change in which citizens protest under uncertainty about the critical crowd required and
     the state sets the severity of repression in advance.
