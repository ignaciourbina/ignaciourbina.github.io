Perceived Relative Automation Risk

Survey item · Ignacio Urbina

Economists and computer scientists estimate how exposed each occupation is to automation. Their methods score the tasks that make up a job on how readily current technology performs them, and the scores rank every occupation in the U.S. workforce from least to most automatable. The rankings place workers on a single national line: some jobs sit near the front, safer than almost all others, and some sit near the back, likely to be automated sooner than almost all others. The item below asks respondents where they believe their own job stands on that line.

Imagine every worker in the country standing on one long line, sorted by how likely their job is to be automated.

The very front of the line (position 0) belongs to people whose jobs are safer than almost everyone else’s, while the very back (position 100) belongs to those whose jobs are at risk sooner than almost everyone else’s.
Someone standing in the middle of the line (position 50) would have half the number of workers ahead of them and half behind.

  • Safest jobs are those whose chances of being automated are below most other people.
  • Riskier jobs are those with an automation potential above most other people.

Taking a moment to reflect on your own job, where do you feel you would place yourself on that line right now?
