Theory & hypotheses

Three traditions converge: personal risk information should increase policy demand.

Self-interest theory, threat-motivated cognition, and egotropic economic voting each predict that learning one's occupation faces automation risk should increase support for protective policies.

Self-interest. Workers facing losses seek compensatory policy (Meltzer & Richard 1981). Threat-motivated cognition. Threat perception focuses attention on protection (Albertson & Gadarian 2015). Egotropic voting. Individual economic exposure drives policy demand (Kinder & Kiewiet 1981).

The two-gate structure predicts three distinct pathways

PathwayWhoWhat happens
1. Signal discountedMid/low trustThe expert estimate is not absorbed; no downstream processing regardless of anxiety or personal exposure
2. Cool cognitionTrusters outside the cornerThe signal is absorbed and beliefs update, but exposure is insufficient to initiate policy search
3. Activated persuasionTrusters inside the cornerBoth gates cleared; sustained elaboration of threat translates into demand for protective policy

Why trust comes first

Gate 1 is about absorption, not agreement: a respondent who discounts the source has nothing to update on. Trust is therefore recovered as a principal stratum from pre-treatment covariates, not as a raw post-treatment split.

Why the second gate is conjunctive

Gate 2 is conjunctive by construction: the prediction is a corner, not two main effects. Neither trait anxiety nor own-occupation automation risk alone should be sufficient to move policy demand. Below the threshold, beliefs update without policy preference change.

Prediction

If the three pathways are mixed in the population, the unconditional ATE will understate the responsive subgroup, producing the weak or null aggregate effects observed in prior work.

Hypotheses

H1 · Trust gate

An expert warning about automation changes policy preferences only among people who credit the source. Those who discount it have nothing to update on, so nothing downstream follows for them.

H2 · Joint activation

Among people who credit the source, the warning changes preferences only where a standing disposition to worry meets genuine personal exposure to the threat. Neither the disposition nor the exposure should be sufficient on its own.

H3 · Composition

Because a population mixes people who stop at each gate with people who pass both, an effect averaged over everyone understates the group that actually responds. Aggregate estimates are then a fact about composition as much as about persuasion.

The pathways are derived from the gate structure rather than from the data.
