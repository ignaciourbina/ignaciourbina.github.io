Theory & hypotheses

When do citizens demand state protection in response to expert warnings about automation risk?

The Conditional Activation Model answers with two sequential gates.

  • H1 · Trust gate.Expert warnings raise policy demand only among respondents who credit the source. Among respondents who do not, the effect is zero.
  • H2 · Joint activation.Among respondents who credit the source, the effect is largest where trait anxiety and own-occupation risk are both high. Neither anxiety nor risk alone produces it.
  • H3 · Composition.The average effect understates the effect in the responding subgroup, because it averages over respondents for whom the gates did not clear.

Theory & hypotheses

When do citizens demand state protection in response to expert warnings about automation risk?

Three traditions make the same prediction

Self-interest theory, threat-motivated cognition and egotropic economic voting predict the same thing. A worker who learns that their occupation faces automation risk should support protective policy more.

Self-interest. Workers facing losses seek compensatory policy (Meltzer & Richard 1981). Threat-motivated cognition. Threat perception focuses attention on protection (Albertson & Gadarian 2015). Egotropic voting. Individual economic exposure drives policy demand (Kinder & Kiewiet 1981).

The two-gate structure predicts three distinct pathways

PathwayWhoWhat happens
1. Signal discountedLower trust in the sourceThe expert estimate is not absorbed. Anxiety and exposure then make no difference
2. Cool cognitionTrust the source, but outside the cornerThe signal is absorbed and beliefs update. Exposure is too low to change policy preferences
3. Activated persuasionTrust the source, and in the cornerBoth gates clear. The threat stays salient and raises demand for protective policy

Why trust comes first

Gate 1 governs absorption, not agreement. A respondent who discounts the source has nothing to update on. Trust is therefore recovered as a principal stratum from pre-treatment covariates. The raw post-treatment item never splits the sample.

Why the second gate is conjunctive

Gate 2 is conjunctive by construction. The prediction is a corner, not two main effects. Neither trait anxiety nor own-occupation risk alone should move policy demand. Below the threshold, beliefs update but policy preferences do not.

Prediction

Mixing the three pathways makes the unconditional ATE understate the responsive subgroup. That produces the weak or null averages reported in prior work.

Hypotheses

H1 · Trust gate

An expert warning changes policy preferences only among people who credit the source. People who discount it have nothing to update on, so the warning never reaches them.

H2 · Joint activation

Among people who credit the source, the warning changes preferences only where a standing disposition to worry meets real personal exposure. Neither one alone should be enough.

H3 · Composition

A population mixes people who stop at each gate with people who pass both. An effect averaged over everyone therefore understates the group that responds. Average estimates report composition as much as persuasion.

The pathways follow from the gate structure, not from the data.
