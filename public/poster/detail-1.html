The puzzle · expanded

Experimental results are inconsistent: null, positive, and opposing effects coexist.

Studies examining conditional effects find heterogeneity that washes out in the aggregate.

Prior evidence

  • Null aggregate effects are common.Zhang & Hou (2022), Magistro et al. (2024): personalized risk information → null ATE on policy preferences.
  • Positive findings exist but with different designs.Gallego et al. (2022): distinct treatment and sample yield positive effects.
  • Subgroup heterogeneity drives cancellation.Arntz et al. (2024): opposing subgroup effects cancel in the aggregate. Wu (2022), Im (2023): effects moderated by ideology, education, prior beliefs.

Reframing the estimand

The unconditional ATE averages over heterogeneous subgroups, so the estimand is misspecified for the question at hand. Weak aggregate effects may reflect composition rather than preference stability. The relevant estimand conditions on who absorbs the signal and on who is personally exposed.

Standard estimand

E[Y(1) − Y(0)]: unconditional ATE across the full sample

Proposed estimand

E[Y(1) − Y(0) | trust stratum, anxiety × automation risk]: CATE by expert-trust stratum and by joint exposure

What follows from the reframing

A weak population-level ATE is the expected signature when qualitatively distinct pathways are mixed, rather than an anomaly to be explained away. In the present study the aggregate effect is not itself null: it is positive but modest, and it understates the group that moves.
