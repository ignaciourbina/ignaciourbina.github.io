Gate 1 — trust-stratum estimates

The treated arm splits on the observed trust rating. For each stratum, entropy balancing reweights the control arm to exact mean equality on 18 pre-treatment covariates, and the effect is the treated-stratum mean minus the reweighted control mean. Exact balance makes residual covariate adjustment identically zero, so no adjusted variant exists.

Gate 2 — the fixed conjunctive corner

A respondent sits in the corner if trait anxiety and automation risk each sit at or above the pooled sample's median, both measured pre-treatment: trait anxiety z ≥ 0.06 and automation risk ≥ 32.1st percentile. Membership follows deterministically from the data. Only the treatment effect is estimated, inside and outside the corner, by weighted least squares.

Cutoff-choice sensitivity

The same model was fit at three pre-specified cutoffs: the median (50th percentile, primary), the 58.3rd percentile, and the tercile (66.7th). The higher-trust corner total's 95% CI excludes zero at every cutoff. The increment δ is directionally stable, with P(>0) between 0.946 and 0.958 across cutoffs. Its 95% CI includes zero at each cutoff, so the increment is the underpowered quantity. Lower-trust δ stays null throughout.

How badly would mean ignorability have to fail?

These estimates fall to zero only under a hidden baseline difference of +0.58 SD for the higher-trust effect, or +0.28 SD for the stratum contrast: the reweighted controls would have to understate the higher-trust stratum's no-treatment outcome by that much despite exact balance on 18 covariates.

All intervals are bootstrap percentile intervals, B = 2000, with every estimation step refit inside each replicate.
