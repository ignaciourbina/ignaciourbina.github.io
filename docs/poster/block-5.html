Two methods compute the MDE and they agree closely. One is a normal-approximation formula. The other simulates a power curve, resampling the real data and injecting a candidate true effect on top of the actual covariate distribution and residual structure. The corner effects are better powered than the increments δ, because they are larger against essentially the same noise.

Robustness

  • Covariate adjustment.Higher trust 0.329, lower trust 0.110, difference 0.219. The ordering holds, modestly attenuated.
  • The cutoff choice does not drive the corner.δ's 95% CI excludes zero at the median, the 58.3rd percentile and the tercile.
  • Nothing is fitted except the treatment effect.Corner membership is a deterministic function of two pre-treatment variables, so no cutoff or sharpness parameter is estimated.

Sensitivity to the identifying assumption

Principal ignorability identifies the trust-stratum estimates. This design cannot test it, because expert trust was never measured in the control arm. The breakdown values are −1.17 SD for the higher-trust effect and −0.52 SD for the difference between the two. Only a hidden differential response that large, and pointing against the mechanism under study, would drive the estimates to zero.

The design that would resolve it

Measuring expert trust before treatment estimates the missing control-arm quantity directly.
