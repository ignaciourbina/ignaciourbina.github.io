Theory & hypotheses · expanded

The two-gate structure predicts three qualitatively distinct cognitive pathways.

Each pathway corresponds to a different exit point in the information-processing sequence.

PathwayWhoWhat happens
1. Signal discountedMid/low trustThe expert estimate is not absorbed; no downstream processing regardless of anxiety or personal exposure
2. Cool cognitionTrusters outside the cornerThe signal is absorbed and beliefs update, but exposure is insufficient to initiate policy search
3. Activated persuasionTrusters inside the cornerBoth gates cleared; sustained elaboration of threat translates into demand for protective policy

Why trust comes first

Gate 1 is about absorption, not agreement: a respondent who discounts the source has nothing to update on. Trust is therefore recovered as a principal stratum from pre-treatment covariates, not as a raw post-treatment split.

Why the second gate is conjunctive

Gate 2 is conjunctive by construction: the prediction is a corner, not two main effects. Neither trait anxiety nor own-occupation automation risk alone should be sufficient to move policy demand.

Prediction

If the three pathways are mixed in the population, the unconditional ATE will understate the responsive subgroup, producing the weak or null aggregate effects observed in prior work.

Hypotheses

H1 · Trust gate

The treatment effect on policy demand is larger among respondents who trust the expert source than among those who do not.

H2 · Conjunctive activation

Among those who absorb the signal, the effect concentrates where high trait anxiety meets high own-occupation automation risk, rather than where either is high alone.

H3 · Composition

The unconditional ATE understates the responsive subgroup, so aggregate estimates are a fact about composition as well as about persuasion.

The pathways are derived from the gate structure rather than from the data.
