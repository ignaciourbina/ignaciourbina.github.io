Takeaways & implications

What the two-gate account offers the literature.

Theoretical

A two-gate model accounts for conflicting prior results as composition across pathways rather than as preference stability. Where a weak or null average is read as evidence that risk information does not move policy preferences, the model treats that average as a mixture over respondents who never absorbed the signal, respondents who absorbed it without acting on it, and respondents for whom both gates cleared. Because both gates are specified in advance and measured before treatment, the reframing is testable rather than rhetorical.

Methodological

Expert trust is post-treatment and observed in the treated arm only. A pre-treatment proxy plus principal-score weighting recovers stratum effects from both arms under an explicit, sensitivity-tested assumption, rather than splitting the treated arm and comparing it to an unsplit control.

Implication

Risk communication that ignores who trusts the source, and who is personally exposed, will understate responsiveness in exactly the group that responds.

Scope

  • Sample.N = 901 U.S. online workers, 62% Democratic including leaners.
  • Trust measurement.A single post-treatment item, asked in the treated arm only. This is why the trust results rest on principal ignorability.
  • Timing.Outcomes measured immediately.
  • Risk estimates.Occupational automation risk follows Frey & Osborne (2017).
  • Power.The difference between them and both corner increments fall below their own minimum detectable effects.

Where this goes

A design measuring expert trust before treatment would resolve the identification question directly, by estimating the control-arm quantity the present design leaves unobserved. The two-gate architecture itself generalizes to any persuasion domain with expert sources and dispositional moderators, including climate, vaccines and trade.
