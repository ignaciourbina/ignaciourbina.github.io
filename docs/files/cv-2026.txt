1. Ignacio Urbina. The Conditional Activation Model: Uncovering Policy Demand Responses to AI-Driven
Automation (Job Market Paper).
• Introduces a two-gate framework explaining when citizens update policy preferences in response to AI-driven

job automation risk. Pre-registered experiment with 901 U.S. workers; trust-stratum effects recovered by entropy
balancing on pre-treatment covariates.

2. Ignacio Urbina. Simulating Open Democracy: An Agent-Based Framework for Testing Deliberative Institutional Designs.
• Open-source simulation using LLM agents as synthetic citizens to test how different institutional rules impact

democratic consensus. Presented at the 2026 MPSA Meeting.

3. Ignacio Urbina & Oleg Smirnov. One Dimension or Many? The Structure of Artificial Intelligence Attitudes
in Mass Publics.
• Maps the structure of U.S. public attitudes toward AI with exploratory factor analysis on a nationally representative

sample (N = 5,153): fifty-two items resolve into ten factors organized by the social context of deployment, a beliefsystem network with no general-attitude hub, and demographic cleavages that reverse direction across dimensions.

2. Ignacio Urbina. Automation Risk, Redistribution, and Economic Vulnerability: Evidence from the European
Social Survey, 2002–2022
• Rebuilds and extends the Thewissen and Rueda (2019) design across all eleven European Social Survey waves (N >

275,000 in 39 countries). The automation risk association with redistribution preferences reappears in 2002–2012
but is smaller in the following decade, with wide country-year variation.
• Welfare and labor market institutions condition the exposure–insecurity link: union density weakens it, while net
income inequality and strict employment protection strengthen it.

3. Ignacio Urbina. Repressive Democracies: The Relationship Between State’s Repression Severity, Protests, and
Policy Change.
• A global game of policy change in which citizens protest under uncertainty about the critical crowd required

and the state sets the severity of repression in advance. The unique threshold equilibrium delivers unconditional
comparative statics, and the policy prize drops out of the participation margin.
• An anger extension shows that backlash is an elasticity condition on the motivational response to severity and that
outrage caps the state’s optimal repression.
